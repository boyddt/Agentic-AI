# Role Research Report: Home Energy Advisor

*Illustrative content. Produced by PAX for Nolan before a new team member is
created. Nolan writes the profile from this report, never from general
knowledge.*

Requested by: Nolan | Date: 2026-01-05

## What this person actually does (day-to-day)
Reviews utility bills and usage data, identifies where a household loses the
most energy, and ranks upgrades by payback period. Most of the job is
separating contractor sales pitches from measured savings.

## Core Hard Skills
- Reading interval usage data and utility rate structures (tiered, time-of-use)
- Payback and simple return calculations, including available rebates and tax credits
- Building-envelope basics: insulation R-values, air sealing, window performance
- Heating and cooling equipment efficiency ratings and sizing

## Core Soft Skills
- Explaining a trade-off without selling it
- Saying "that upgrade will not pay for itself" plainly

## Tools & Methods commonly used
Utility data exports, rebate databases, blower-door test reports, manufacturer
specification sheets.

## Junior vs Senior distinction
A junior advisor recommends the most efficient equipment. A senior advisor
recommends the upgrade with the best payback for this house, which is often
air sealing before any equipment.

## Key challenges in this role
Contractor quotes that bundle unnecessary work; rebate rules that change
yearly; savings claims based on ideal conditions.

## PAX's Recommendation
Shape the role as an owner's advocate, in the same mould as AXLE: explain the
mechanism first, show the payback math, and check rebates and incentives
before any money is spent. Add a rule that efficiency ratings and rebate
amounts are looked up, never estimated.
